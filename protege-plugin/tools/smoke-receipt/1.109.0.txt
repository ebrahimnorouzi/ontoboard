A SPREADSHEET CAN BE PERFECT AND STILL BE WRONG

Template... has always answered one question: will this build, and if not, where is the fault.
That is a question about ROBOT. Nobody was asking the other one - is this data any good - and
it turns out to have different answers.

The knowledge graph this was written against builds through ROBOT with ZERO problems. Checked
as data, its 26 sheets and 8,471 rows report:

    687  cells holding text a template left behind - "_name_", "n/a"
    145  cells with pasted whitespace: a doubled space, a tab, a non-breaking space
    144  cells with a curly quote or a long dash where a plain one was meant
    123  references to a name that nothing defines
     77  pairs of rows whose names are one edit apart, with different identifiers
     49  references to a name that means TWO different things
      1  pair of rows sharing one identifier
      1  column with data in it that the template row ignores
    ----
   1227  findings, 289 of them carrying a correction that can be applied

Six of the 26 sheets are completely clean. The whole audit takes about six seconds.

WHAT IT IS CHECKING AGAINST

A folder, not a sheet. Every reference in that project's dataportal sheet is a plain-text name
whose target is defined five sheets upstream, so checking one sheet alone reports almost every
reference as unresolvable. All the sheets in the folder, plus the ontology that is open, are
indexed together first - 6,058 distinct names - and the sheets are checked against that.

THE INTERESTING FINDING IS AMBIGUITY, NOT ABSENCE

Of those 6,058 names, 30 name more than one thing. "National Institute for Materials Science
(NIMS)" has two distinct IRIs and nine references name it; "IUC 05: Towards better digital
infrastructures and workflows for experimental labs" names three things. ROBOT resolves such a
name to whichever IRI it happens to pick, silently, and the author has no say - so the sheet
builds and the graph is wrong. LabelIndex.resolve therefore returns nothing at all when a name
is ambiguous, because returning one of two would be the behaviour the class exists to expose.

THE CHECK THAT NEARLY SHIPPED UNUSABLE

The near-duplicate check, as first written, reported 10,059 findings on the temporal sheet
alone - and every one was wrong. That sheet names its rows "temporal region 1" through
"temporal region 171", so each is a single edit from several others. A serial name is the point
rather than a typo.

Stripping the digits and comparing what is left separates "temporal region 1" from "temporal
region 2" without losing "Stuttgart" from "Stuttgart_" or "Fraunhofer-Gesellschaft" from
"Fraunhofer-Gesellschafft". That one rule took the count from 10,395 to 77, and the 77 are
real. A check that cries wolf is one somebody turns off, so a named test asserts that a
numbered series is not reported.

Two more deliberate silences, each with a test: names shorter than six characters are not
compared at all, because "iron" and "zinc" are two edits apart; and two similar names on ONE
identifier are a thing with two names rather than two things.

WHAT IT REFUSES TO DECIDE

A finding carries a replacement only where there is an obvious one - whitespace, a curly quote,
a quoted name, a near-miss reference with exactly one close candidate. Which of two duplicated
entities to keep is not a decision a tool should make, so those findings carry no suggestion
and say what the choice is instead. 289 of 1,227 are applicable; the rest need a person.

THE MENU SAID WHERE THESE THINGS BELONG, AND IT WAS WRONG

Adding Check sheets... to ROBOT made that group seven entries, and the test added in 1.105.0
failed with "ontoboard.menu.OntoBoard.robot/SlotB has 7 entries with no separator between
them". That is exactly what it was written to catch - an append instead of a decision - and it
caught the person who wrote it.

Building a knowledge graph from spreadsheets is something somebody sets out to do, not a ROBOT
utility reached for in passing. So Template... and Check sheets... are now a Knowledge graph
menu of their own, and Pattern library... sits beside it at the top level for the same reason:
both are ways of getting content in, neither is about running a command. ROBOT keeps the ten
things that are: inspect (6), terms in and out (2), change in bulk (2).

NUMBERS

2033 tests, 0 failures. 45 new - 20 for the index, 25 for the audit. No main-source visibility
was widened.

42/42 classes named in plugin.xml load on both hosts, up from 41. 163 patterns indexed. Quality
report end to end: 6 findings, 2 of them errors.

Two new classes carry no OWL API, no Swing and no robot-core between them, so they are cheap
enough to re-run while somebody types - which is what the editor they are for will need.

On 5.5.0, cellfie-2.2.4 and swrltab-plugin-2.1.3 fail to install. Neither is ours.

STILL OPEN

This is the engine, reachable through one read-only menu item. Editing a sheet in place -
adding rows, completing a name as it is typed, applying a correction, minting an identifier,
creating the referenced thing on the spot - is the next stage and is what the index and the
audit were built to serve.

The starter template still produces zero axioms on every real project, and PMD_0000001 and
NFDI_0000001 are live published terms, so the fix cannot keep the hardcoded example
identifiers. That one is specified rather than guessed.
