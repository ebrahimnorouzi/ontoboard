ROBOT > Rename IRIs...

Moving a namespace is the job: an ontology drafted under http://example.org/ that is about to be
published under http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/. Protege renames one entity at a time and cannot
be told "everything under this prefix", so a few hundred terms is an afternoon of clicking and a
real chance of missing one.

Two modes - whole IRIs, or prefixes - previewed before anything is applied, with "Apply the
changes" off by default. The changes go through Protege's model manager, so Edit > Undo reverses
an entire namespace migration in one step. A bulk IRI change is exactly what somebody wants to
look at first and undo second.

Two decisions worth recording, both made because the obvious version was worse:

  ROBOT's three-argument renameFull delegates to the four-argument one with false, which makes it
  throw MISSING ENTITY ERROR and abandon the whole rename the moment one mapping names an IRI the
  ontology does not have. For a list pasted out of a spreadsheet that is the wrong trade. The
  four-argument form with true renames what matches; TermRename then reports the lines that
  matched nothing, because a mapping that silently does nothing is how somebody concludes a rename
  worked when it did not. Found by a test that expected a report and got an exception.

  Mappings parse from "old -> new" or from a tab, because one comes from a text editor and the
  other from a spreadsheet column pair, and refusing either sends somebody off to reformat a list
  by hand. A line with neither separator names its own line number rather than being dropped.

RenameOperation was cleared with javap -v over its whole constant pool - no RDF4J, no openrdf, no
POI.

WHAT WAS DELIBERATELY NOT DONE

UnmergeOperation. It removes one ontology's axioms from another, and inside Protege there is
almost nothing for it to do: the open ontology either declares imports, in which case its own
axioms and its imports' are already separate, or it does not. The only case it addresses is an
ontology that both imports something and duplicates that import's axioms in its own file, which is
rare and which a curator would not think to look for. Wiring it into Transform would also have
needed the copy-with-shadow-import strategy changed, because that shadow deliberately holds
closure-minus-own axioms - so there is nothing duplicated for unmerge to find there either. Left
out rather than added as a menu item that would report "nothing to change" on every ontology
anybody tried it on.
