THE TOOLBAR DID NOT FIT, AND THE STATUS LINE ERASED ITS OWN WARNINGS

The first half of Release 4 of docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-26-canvas-board-design.md. The spec has it
as one release; it is split here, and the reason is in the spec's own regression table: the floating
zoom cluster and minimap are "the most likely breakage in the whole plan", because Delete, Escape,
Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+F are all bound on the graph component's ancestor input map and one focusable button
floating over it silently kills all four. That risk, the shortcuts panel and the pan-gesture inversion go
together in 1.68.0. This release is layout, labels and menus - nothing that can steal focus.

1. THE COLLABORATE BUTTON WAS NOT ON THE SCREEN

Measured with the real components under Java 11: the toolbar wanted 1261px and began clipping at 1030.
The OntoBoard tab in a 1440px Protege window gives it 857, at which "Collaborate..." is laid out at
x=812, width 86 - past the right edge, unpainted and unclickable. JToolBar uses a BoxLayout, which lays
overflowing children out beyond the container rather than wrapping them, and there is no chevron or
overflow to switch on. Nothing reported this. The button was simply not there, and had not been for as
long as the bar has held nine controls.

Three changes bring the preferred width to 777px:

  - The layout combo goes - 201px to choose between four entries nobody reopens - and Arrange becomes a
    button that pops them.
  - Legend, Export and Collaborate move into an overflow menu, along with a Snap to grid toggle. Add
    selected, Add all, Find and Inferences stay out: Inferences is a mode with a visible consequence on
    the diagram, and a mode you can forget you are in must not be hidden.
  - The two widths that were free to grow are pinned. The Find box had no ceiling, so BoxLayout poured
    all of a wide bar's slack into it and it reached 407px; and the Add button is relabelled on every
    selection change, from "Add selected" at 108px to "Add Cheesey vegetable topping" at 211 - so
    clicking through the class tree shifted everything to its right by up to 103px, including the Find
    field, which slid out from under the pointer mid-type. Its label is elided now, and the elision rule
    is a tested pure function rather than an inline substring.

2. ONE STATUS LABEL, TWO PRODUCERS, AND THE WRONG ONE WON

setStatus() writes transient board confirmations - "Arranged the board", "Undid: adding 7 terms". The
collaboration host writes persistent session state that needs acting on - "3 changes not shared",
"Disconnected - you switched ontology". Both wrote the same label, so whichever fired last won, and it
won permanently: arranging the board erased the only notice that a colleague would never see your edit,
and nothing ever brought it back.

Two labels now. The session sits left at a fixed 220px with a coloured dot - green connected, amber
something needs attention, grey working through git - and the board's own line takes the middle. Every
writer was repointed: seven call sites, each to exactly one channel.

3. THE CONTEXT MENU OFFERED AN ACTION THAT LIED ABOUT WHAT IT ACTED ON

Eleven items in three arbitrary groups: the two creation actions pinned above everything while "New class
here" sat five items below, the two axiom-writing items five apart, the two destructive ones neither
adjacent nor last. And "Add selected entity to canvas" was offered even when you had right-clicked a
node, where it reads as "add the thing I clicked" and in fact acts on Protege's tree selection, which may
be something else entirely. That item is deleted.

Four groups now, in the order a person asks: what is under the cursor, what it can be joined to, what can
be made here, and - last, after a separator - what takes something away. Delete carries its accelerator so
the key is taught rather than discovered. Nine items on a term, four on empty canvas.

4. THE EMPTY STATE NAMED A WIDGET THAT DOES NOT EXIST

"Double-click the board to add a class, or drag one in from the palette on the left." There is no board to
double-click - the card is covering it - and there is no palette: the left column of the OntoBoard tab is
Protege's own entity views, the first labelled Classes. Both corrected, a fourth action added ("Add every
term", which already guards at 300), and one line that nothing anywhere said before:

    Taking something off the board never deletes it from the ontology.

That is the single most consequential fact about this canvas, and until now the only place it appeared was
in the status bar after somebody had already pressed Delete.

5. DARK MODE, ANSWERED RATHER THAN THEMED

Protege 5.6 ships a dark look and feel. Nothing inside the canvas breaks - every fill is a near-white tint
with ink at 14.8:1 or better. What broke was everything around it: the board was a bright borderless slab
in a dark workbench, and StartPanel - the first screen anybody sees - hardcoded four light colours and
painted itself the canvas colour.

The canvas stays light on purpose, and the comment says why: it is the surface every exported PNG is
composed on, and a diagram whose colours depend on the author's IDE theme is not reproducible. It gains a
border whose colour follows the theme. StartPanel now takes its colours from the look and feel.

One thing that took two attempts, caught by re-rendering. Taking the card's background from
Panel.background - the obvious token - makes the card exactly the colour of the thing it is supposed to be
raised off, and on the default light theme it came out darker than its own surround: a card pressed into
the page rather than lifted off it. Swing has no "surface" token, so the rule is explicit - white on a
light theme, a step lighter than the panel on a dark one.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT COVER

The toolbar measurements quoted above come from the design review, not from this run; what this run proves
is that the rebuilt toolbar, the two-label status bar and the restructured menu all construct inside both
Protege versions, which is where a missing icon class or a null label would show up. Whether the bar now
fits a 857px tab is a thing to look at.

Also unverified: the dark theme itself. The luminance test and the two colour rules are written and
compile; nobody has run this plugin under a dark look and feel. The design proof renders the empty state
against the default theme only.
